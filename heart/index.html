3. The ECG

One line of the electrocardiogram, scrolling like the paper strip at the vet. Each small square is 0.04 s. P = the atria fire, QRS = the ventricles fire (the big spike), T = the ventricles recharge. In Duque's strap lead the T wave points down.

Each dot is one heartbeat: the time since the previous beat (the RR interval). A perfect metronome would be a flat line. Breathing makes the dots rise and fall (faint blue curve); the random wander x in card 5 adds drift and scatter. SDNN and RMSSD are the two numbers vets and researchers use for heart-rate variability (HRV).

5. The Math Behind It

dθ/dt = ω(t) = 2π · HR(t) / 60

ECG(θ) = Σᵢ aᵢ · exp( −(θ − θᵢ)² / 2bᵢ² ) + baseline

One clock hand (θ) sweeps around once per heartbeat. Each wave is a bump of height aᵢ and width bᵢ sitting at its own angle θᵢ on the clock face: P, Q, R, S, T. The ECG is just the sum of the bumps as the hand passes them. (Model: McSharry, Clifford, Tarassenko & Smith, 2003.)

HR(t) = HR₀ · (1 + RSA · sin 2πφbreath) · (1 + x)

dx = −(x / τ) dt + σ dW

The heart rate is a set point HR₀, swayed by breathing, times a wandering factor x that follows a stochastic differential equation. The first term is a spring: it pulls x back toward 0 with memory time τ. The second term is random kicks from the nervous system (a Wiener process, dW). Switch the kicks off and the spring wins: the wander dies away. This is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, the simplest SDE there is. The wander-size slider sets the long-run standard deviation s of x, so σ = s·√(2/τ).

Rhythm

Short τ with kicks on: beat-to-beat scatter (RMSSD climbs). Long τ: slow drift that lasts many beats (SDNN climbs, RMSSD barely moves). Kicks off: watch the wander decay with time τ, then only the breathing sway remains.

A dropped beat: the atria fire (P wave) but the signal never reaches the ventricles, so no QRS and no T. Fit horses often do this a few times a minute while standing quietly. It should vanish when they move.

Wave shape

Turn rate adaptation off and push the heart rate past 150: the T wave of one beat runs into the P wave of the next. Real hearts avoid this because the ventricles recharge faster at high rates (the QT interval scales with the square root of the RR interval, Bazett's rule).
